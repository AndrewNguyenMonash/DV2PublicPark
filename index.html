The Park Next Door

How Australia's eight capital-city councils share out parks, play spaces and places to gather

By Andrew Nguyen · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2

Think of the park at the end of your street: some grass, a playground, perhaps a barbecue. Local councils look after places like this, where many Australians walk, play and meet their neighbours. This page compares the parks of Australia's eight capital-city councils: how much park land each provides for its residents, and what is in those parks. The gap is wide, from about 8 m² of park per resident in the City of Sydney to over 270 m² in Adelaide and Hobart. Then we zoom in on Brisbane, Australia's largest council, to see where its parks are and when they are booked most.

Notes on the data

Only council-managed parks are included; national parks, state parks and state-run gardens are left out.

Councils publish their data in different years, from 2014 (Adelaide) to 2026. A facility that is not published is not the same as one that does not exist.
